Moving to Korea starts with one important question: **which visa do you need?** Korea
has dozens of visa categories, each with its own code (like D-2 or E-7), purpose, and
requirements. This guide breaks down the most common ones in plain language.

## Study visas

- **D-2 — Degree Student:** For people enrolled in a university program (associate,
  bachelor's, master's, or PhD) at a Korean institution.
- **D-4 — General Trainee / Language Student:** For studying at a Korean language
  institute (한국어학당) or other non-degree training.

If your goal is to study Korean before university, you'll usually start on a **D-4** and
later switch to a **D-2**.

## Work visas

- **E-2 — Foreign Language Instructor:** The classic visa for native English (and other
  language) teachers.
- **E-7 — Special Occupation:** For skilled professionals sponsored by a Korean company
  (IT, engineering, management, and more).
- **E-9 — Non-Professional Employment:** For workers under the Employment Permit System,
  often in manufacturing or agriculture.
- **D-8 — Corporate Investment:** For people running or investing in a business in Korea.
- **D-10 — Job Seeker:** A temporary visa that lets you stay in Korea while looking for
  qualifying work.

## Family and residence visas

- **F-2 — Resident:** A longer-term residence visa, including the points-based F-2-7.
- **F-4 — Overseas Korean:** For people of Korean heritage.
- **F-5 — Permanent Residence:** The long-term goal for many — no need to renew.
- **F-6 — Marriage Migrant:** For spouses of Korean citizens.

## How to choose the right visa

Ask yourself three questions:

1. **What is my main purpose?** Study, work, business, or family?
2. **Who is sponsoring me?** A school, an employer, a spouse, or no one yet?
3. **How long do I plan to stay?** Short-term study, or a path to permanent residence?

Your answers point you toward the right category. From there, each visa has a specific
document checklist — and getting the documents right the first time saves weeks of delay.
