If you're building a life in Korea, you'll hear two acronyms again and again: **KIIP**
and **TOPIK**. They sound similar and both involve Korean language, but they serve
different purposes. Here's how to tell them apart and decide which one you need.

## What is TOPIK?

**TOPIK** (Test of Proficiency in Korean / 한국어능력시험) is a standardized **language
exam**. It measures how well you read, listen, and write in Korean, and gives you a level
from **1 to 6** (6 is highest).

You'll typically need TOPIK for:

- **University admission** (many programs require TOPIK 3–4 or higher)
- **Scholarships**
- **Certain jobs and visas** that ask for proof of Korean ability
- **Points** toward residence visas like the F-2-7

TOPIK is held on fixed exam dates several times a year, and you study for it like any
language test — vocabulary, grammar, reading, and listening practice.

## What is KIIP?

**KIIP** (Korea Immigration & Integration Program / 사회통합프로그램) is a **government
program**, not just a test. It's a series of classes covering the Korean language **and**
Korean society, culture, and law. It ends with an evaluation.

Completing KIIP can give you real benefits:

- **Easier path to residence and naturalization** (it can replace some requirements)
- **Extra points** on points-based visas
- A recognized certificate of social integration

In short: **TOPIK proves your language level; KIIP shows you've completed Korea's official
integration program.**

## Side-by-side

| | **TOPIK** | **KIIP** |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Language proficiency exam | Government integration program |
| Covers | Reading, listening, writing | Language + Korean society & culture |
| Result | Level 1–6 | Program completion + evaluation |
| Mainly for | University, jobs, scholarships | Residence, naturalization, integration |

## Which one should you take?

- **Going to university or need to prove your Korean level?** → **TOPIK**
- **Planning to settle long-term, get residence, or naturalize?** → **KIIP** (and often
  TOPIK too)
- **Not sure?** Many people end up doing **both** at different stages of their journey.
